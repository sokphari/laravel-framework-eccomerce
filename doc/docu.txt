4. User Roles & Permission Model

Every server-side endpoint checks authorization; hiding buttons in React is not sufficient. Sensitive actions can require reauthentication and optional 2FA.

6. Module Functional Requirements
01. Identity & RBAC
Registration/login, JWT, roles, permissions, device logout. Database: users, roles, permissions, user_roles, role_permissions.
02. Customer Management
Profiles, address book, order relationships. Database: customer_profiles, customer_addresses.
03. Categories & Brands
Hierarchical categories, brand logos, slugs. Database: categories, brands.
04. Product Management
SKU, variants, media, draft/published lifecycle. Database: products, product_images, product_variants.
Products can be parent models with variants for color/storage; product_images reference media assets, variants have unique SKU and explicit stock policy.
05. Dynamic Specifications
RAM, storage, color, battery, connectivity. Database: attributes, attribute_options, category_attributes, product_attribute_values.
06. Second-Hand Products
Per-unit condition, grade, photos, inspection checklist. Database: product_units, product_conditions, inspection_reports.
Second-hand phone units must reference a variant, grade/condition, inspection results, unit-specific media, acquisition cost, sale status and optional serial entry.
07. Inventory & Stock
Per-warehouse inventory, movement ledger and reservations. Database: warehouses, stocks, stock_movements, stock_reservations.
08. Serial & IMEI Tracking
Unique device identity, status and order assignment. Database: product_serials.
IMEI/serial must be unique (when applicable); store normalized identifiers securely and control staff visibility. Avoid public exposure of full IMEI.
09. Free Gifts & Bundles
Gift eligibility, bundle pricing, stock deduction. Database: gift_promotions, gift_promotion_items, order_gifts, product_bundles, bundle_items.
Gift rules define dates, eligible cart items, gift stock, max quantity, stacking and cancellation/return recovery. Each gifted item becomes a zero-charge order_gift or order line with attribution.
10. Pricing Management
Effective date, currency, price approval/audit. Database: product_prices, price_histories.
11. Discounts & Promotions
Campaign rules, caps, eligibility and stacking priority. Database: promotions, promotion_rules, promotion_redemptions.
12. Shopping Cart
Quantity edits, server-side price revalidation. Database: carts, cart_items.
13. Wishlist & Compare
Save and compare phones/specs. Database: wishlists, wishlist_items, product_comparisons.
14. Orders
Checkout, state transitions and snapshot prices. Database: orders, order_items, order_status_histories.
Order items snapshot SKU, name, variant, tax, discounts, quantity and price at checkout; subsequent catalog edits must not alter historical orders.
15. Payment Management
Gateway reconciliation, webhook verification, idempotency. Database: payments, payment_transactions, refunds.
Only confirmed verified payment webhook + reconciliation can mark paid; never trust frontend-reported success.
16. Delivery & Shipping
Delivery options, tracking, fulfillment events. Database: shipping_methods, shipments, shipment_tracking.
17. Returns & Exchanges
RMA lifecycle, restock decisions and refunds. Database: return_requests, return_items, exchanges.
18. Warranty Management
Device IMEI-linked warranty and service claims. Database: warranties, warranty_claims.
19. Reviews & Ratings
Verified purchaser reviews and moderation. Database: product_reviews, review_images.
20. Coupons & Loyalty
Coupon rules, points ledger, redemption controls. Database: coupons, coupon_usages, loyalty_accounts, loyalty_transactions.
21. Suppliers & Purchasing
Supply acquisition, goods received and cost. Database: suppliers, purchase_orders, purchase_order_items.
22. Notifications
Email, in-app; future Telegram and real-time. Database: notifications, notification_templates.
23. Reports & Analytics
Sales/stock/returns metrics and background exports. Database: report_exports, dashboard_snapshots.
24. System & Audit
Config, immutable activity evidence and media metadata. Database: audit_logs, settings, media_files.
Audit actor, action, entity type/ID, before/after safe diffs, IP, request ID and timestamp. Never log raw JWT, passwords or payment credentials.
7. Data Model & Relationship Design
    • users ↔ roles: many-to-many through user_roles; roles ↔ permissions: many-to-many through role_permissions.
    • categories → products, brands → products, products → variants, products → images, categories ↔ attributes.
    • product_variants → stocks (per warehouse), stock_movements, stock_reservations; serials/product_units reference a specific variant.
    • carts → cart_items → product_variants; orders → order_items → product_variants (nullable FK if archived, snapshot preserved).
    • orders → payments → payment_transactions; orders → shipments; order_items → warranties / returns.
    • gift_promotions ↔ applicable products; order_gifts → orders and allocated gift variant; bundles ↔ variants.
    • suppliers → purchase_orders → purchase_order_items → received stock movements.
    • audit_logs → actor user, entity type/ID; notifications → recipient user.
Recommended common fields
id (UUID/ULID or bigint chosen consistently), created_at, updated_at, optional deleted_at, status, created_by/updated_by where applicable. Monetary fields as BIGINT minor units (with currency) or NUMERIC precision; never floating point. Use UNIQUE, FOREIGN KEY and CHECK constraints, and transaction boundaries.
Phone-specific data examples
Field / entity	Purpose
product_variants.sku	Unique sellable model configuration (e.g., 128GB / Blue).
product_units.condition_id	Grade for second-hand individual units.
product_serials.imei1 / imei2	Optional device IMEI identifiers with controlled access.
inspection_reports	Battery health, screen, camera, sensors, accessories, defects.
stocks.on_hand / reserved	Derived available = on_hand − reserved; enforce invariants.
order_items.unit_price_minor	Price captured at the moment of checkout.
stock_reservations.expires_at	Timed allocation prevents unpaid carts locking stock forever.

8. System Architecture
Recommended initial architecture: Modular Monolith, independently deployable React frontend and Laravel API. This reduces distributed-system complexity while preserving clear module boundaries.
Component	Data flow
React Storefront / Admin	HTTPS → Nginx / reverse proxy → Laravel API
Laravel API	Auth middleware → Rate limiter → Controller → Service/Policy → PostgreSQL
Redis	Cache lookups, rate-limit state, job queue, short-lived locks
Background Workers	Redis jobs → email/in-app notifications, report exports, alerts
Storage	Signed uploads/read URLs for product media; secure private docs
Monitoring	Structured app logs → aggregator, metrics, alerts and health checks

Suggested Docker services
nginx, app (PHP-FPM), queue-worker, scheduler, postgres, redis, frontend-dev. For production use managed PostgreSQL/Redis or backed-up stateful volumes, proper secrets management, TLS termination, independent worker scaling, restart policies and health checks.
9. Checkout, Inventory & Payment Safety
    1. Customer builds cart; server retrieves real SKU, price, gift and promotion eligibility.
    2. Validate address, shipping, coupons, taxes and final totals on server.
    3. Start PostgreSQL transaction; lock relevant stock rows in deterministic order using SELECT ... FOR UPDATE.
    4. Check available stock; create stock reservations and order/order_items atomically; commit.
    5. Create payment intent with unique idempotency key, mapped to order, outside long-lived DB locks.
    6. Verify signed payment webhook and ensure deduplication before marking order paid.
    7. Commit stock movement / assigned IMEI / fulfillment according to defined capture workflow.
    8. For failure or expiry, safely release reservation exactly once; retries remain idempotent.
Important: inventory ledger, reservations and stock summary must use a single explicit consistency model. Free gifts and bundles consume inventory like regular products. Lock rows in stable order to reduce deadlocks; retry transient conflicts safely. Never keep a database transaction open during remote gateway requests.
10. API Standards & Pagination
Base: /api/v1. JSON consistent shape with data, meta and errors. Use Laravel Form Requests for validation, API Resources for serialization, policies for access checks, trace/request IDs and versioning.
Use case	API behavior
Catalog	GET /api/v1/products?search=&brand_id=&category_id=&sort=&per_page=
Product details	GET /api/v1/products/{slug}; variants, specs, gallery, gifts.
Account	POST /api/v1/auth/register; POST /login; POST /refresh; POST /logout.
Cart	GET /cart; POST /cart/items; PATCH /cart/items/{id}; DELETE /cart/items/{id}.
Checkout	POST /checkout with idempotency key; GET /orders/{id} scoped to user.
Payments	POST /webhooks/payments/{provider} (verified signature, replay protection).
Admin products	GET/POST /admin/products; PATCH/DELETE /admin/products/{id}.
Admin inventory	GET /admin/stocks; POST /admin/stock-adjustments with approvals.

Pagination: cap per_page (e.g., 100 maximum, default 20), whitelist sortable/filterable fields, use simplePaginate when total count is unnecessary, and cursorPaginate for huge stable sorted datasets (unique tiebreaker). Index frequently filtered columns; consider full-text/trigram search for product names and specifications. Never interpolate raw input into SQL ORDER BY.
11. API Security & Suggested Rate Limits
Endpoint	Suggested policy	Key scope
Login	5 attempts / minute	Normalized account + IP; additional abuse checks
Register	3 requests / hour	IP, with anti-bot and abuse review
Public products	120 requests / minute	IP or trusted client identification
Search	60 requests / minute	IP/user as applicable
Cart	60 requests / minute	Authenticated user
Checkout	5 requests / minute	Authenticated user + idempotency
Admin APIs	60 requests / minute	Authenticated user

These are initial design targets—not production-proven limits. Tune with telemetry, reverse-proxy controls and legitimate usage. Return HTTP 429 with Retry-After where appropriate; use Redis-backed rate limit store across app instances. Password policies, email verification, reset tokens, short JWT expiry, refresh-token rotation/revocation, optional Admin/Manager 2FA, device/session records, suspicious login monitoring and lockout safeguards are required.
Protect CORS with an exact allowlist, enforce HTTPS, parameterized queries, output encoding and content security policy. For Bearer JWT in Authorization headers, CSRF risk differs from cookie auth; if refresh tokens live in cookies, implement SameSite/Secure/HttpOnly and CSRF protection appropriate to the chosen flow. Never store long-lived sensitive tokens in localStorage by default.
12. Redis Cache & Queues
Cached item	Strategy	Invalidate on
Categories / Brands	TTL 15–60 min; keys by locale and visibility	Create/update/delete or visibility change
Public product summaries	Short TTL 1–10 min; filtered keys	Product, variant, image, price, promotion change
Featured products	TTL 5–15 min	Feature/price/publish/promotion change
Dashboard summaries	TTL 1–5 min or scheduled aggregation	Relevant business events / refresh
Stock & payment state	Strong consistency path, not naive cached source of truth	Verified transactions and movement events

Queues: order confirmation, receipt email, low-stock alerts, report exports, notification dispatch, image processing. Jobs must be retry-safe, use failure handling/dead-letter strategy and monitoring. Laravel Events/Listeners decouple business actions from notifications. Optional future Telegram and Laravel Reverb real-time updates.
13. Audit, Logs, Monitoring & Recovery
    • Audit product create/update/delete, price override, role/permission change, stock receipt/adjustment, refund approvals and order transitions.
    • Use JSON structured logs with timestamp, service, level, request_id, user_id (where safe), endpoint and safe error context.
    • Expose liveness /health/live and readiness /health/ready; avoid exposing credentials/config publicly.
    • Metrics: p95 API latency, 4xx/5xx, payment webhook failures, slow queries, queue lag, cache hit rates, oversell conflicts and stock discrepancies.
    • Alerts: elevated error rates, checkout failure, low inventory, failed jobs and database health.
    • Daily backup + tested restore, defined RPO/RTO, encrypted backups, migration rollback and incident runbooks.

19. Risk Register
Risk	Mitigation
Overselling / duplicate payments	Row locks, reservations, idempotency keys, verified webhooks, replay tests.
JWT leaks / role escalation	Short token lifetimes, rotation, revocation, policies and authorization tests.
Stale price or gift logic	Validate at checkout, snapshot purchases and invalidate event-driven caches.
IMEI exposure or mismatch	Encryption/masking strategy, unique constraints, controlled access and reconciliation.
Unexpected scope growth	Phase gates, explicit business decisions and API contracts.
Operational outage	Observability, backups, restore drills, queue resilience and health checks.
